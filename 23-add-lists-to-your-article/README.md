# Add lists to your article

Theoretical Recap HTML lists are fundamental elements for structuring and presenting related information. They offer semantic meaning to content, enhancing accessibility and SEO. This workshop builds directly on your understanding of headings and paragraphs to create well-organized articles. You will work with three primary list types, defined by their elements. Unordered lists (ul) create bulleted points for items without a specific sequence. Ordered lists (ol) create numbered sequences for procedural or ranked items. Description lists (dl) group terms (dt) with their descriptions (dd). A common mistake is nesting list elements improperly, as each list item (li) is a direct child of ul or ol. The mental model is that of a container. The ul or ol tag establishes the type of list, and each li inside is a discrete item. For description lists, think of term-definition pairs. A clear hierarchy is essential for both structure and screen readers. Another frequent error is using paragraphs where a list is semantically more appropriate. As a mini example, a simple unordered shopping list is coded as: EggsMilk. An ordered recipe step is: Mix ingredientsBake for 30 minutes. Remember, you can nest lists by placing a new ul or ol inside an li. Workshop Brief Your workshop task is to expand an existing article with structured list content.
