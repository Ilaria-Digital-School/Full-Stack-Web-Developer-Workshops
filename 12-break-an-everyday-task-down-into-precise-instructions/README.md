# Break an everyday task down into precise instructions

Theoretical Recap: From Thought to Action In development, thinking like a programmer is as crucial as the code you write. This mindset hinges on algorithmic thinking and decomposition. Algorithmic thinking is a step-by-step logical process for solving a problem. Decomposition is the act of breaking a complex task into smaller, manageable sub-tasks. A clear mental model for this is the concept of imperative instructions. You provide a sequence of unambiguous commands that a computer—or a person with no context—can follow precisely. It requires defining inputs, describing the exact process, and specifying the desired output. This eliminates assumptions and ambiguity. A common mistake is providing high-level or vague steps, like 'make a sandwich'. This lacks the necessary precision. Another error is forgetting to define pre-conditions (e.g., having the ingredients) and post-conditions (e.g., a completed sandwich ready to eat). A mini-example: Step 1 in 'boil water' is '1. Pour 250ml of tap water into a kettle.' This specifies the quantity, source, and tool, making the instruction executable.
