# Assign Scrum accountabilities in a team of four developers

Scrum accountabilities are distinct, clearly defined roles within the Scrum framework designed to foster an effective product development process. In Scrum, there are three key accountabilities: the Product Owner, the Scrum Master, and the Developers. The Product Owner is accountable for maximizing the value of the product resulting from the work of the Developers. They manage the Product Backlog. The Scrum Master is accountable for establishing Scrum as defined in the Scrum Guide. They do this by helping everyone understand Scrum theory and practice, both within the Scrum Team and the organization. The Developers are accountable for creating a usable Increment every Sprint. They self-manage their work and are cross-functional. A common mistake is to confuse these accountabilities with job titles or traditional hierarchical roles. The Product Owner is not a project manager, and the Scrum Master is not the team's boss. Another mistake is to assign the Scrum Master role to the most senior developer as an additional duty, which often leads to neglected Scrum Master responsibilities. In Scrum, accountabilities are not necessarily full-time roles, especially in smaller teams, but each accountability must be fulfilled. For example, in a five-person team, one person might serve as Product Owner, another as Scrum Master, and all five, including the Scrum Master, serve as Developers, contributing to the product Increment.
