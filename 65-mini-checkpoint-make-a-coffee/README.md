# Mini-checkpoint - Make a coffee

THEORETICAL RECAP An algorithm is a finite, ordered sequence of unambiguous instructions designed to solve a specific problem or accomplish a well-defined task. Three foundational concepts underpin everything you have studied so far: sequence (instructions executed one after another, in strict order), clarity (each step must be explicit and leave no room for interpretation), and granularity (steps must be broken down to the right level of detail — neither too vague nor unnecessarily micro-detailed). A flowchart is a visual representation of this sequence, using standardized shapes: rectangles for actions, diamonds for decisions, and arrows to show the flow. Pseudo-code is the written counterpart — structured like code, but readable by any human regardless of programming language. Common mistakes beginners make include: skipping implicit steps (assuming the reader 'knows' something), mixing abstraction levels (some steps too vague, others too granular), forgetting to handle edge cases (what if there is no water? no coffee?), and writing instructions that are not actually sequential (grouping actions that depend on one another without showing that dependency). A simple mental model: imagine you are writing instructions for a robot that knows absolutely nothing. Every action must be spelled out. For example, 'make a hot drink' is too vague. 'Fill the water tank with 200ml of cold water, then press the power button' is appropriately granular.
